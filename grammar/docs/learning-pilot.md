# Usability and learning pilot

## Walkthrough with learners

Recruit Arabic-speaking learners studying B1.1 and B1.2, including people who mainly use a phone and people preparing for telc. Ask participants to use the app as they normally would. Use fresh browser profiles for the start task and seeded practice or drafts for the returning tasks.

| Task to read aloud | Observe |
| --- | --- |
| You have started B1.2. Find a rule about the order of two past events and try it. | First action, wrong turns, time to the first answer, and whether the learner can paraphrase the lesson goal. |
| You stopped in the middle of practice yesterday. Continue, make one correction, and find today's review. | Whether the learner can resume the correct item, identify what the feedback means and find a next action without a prompt. |
| Use the first exam model to write and save the beginning of a reply. Leave the page, return to your draft, and find out how to improve it. | Draft recovery, ability to locate the checklist and worked reply, and understanding of what the exam trainer scores. |
| Find what you have practised recently and download a backup. | Whether the learner distinguishes explanation viewing, practice, and delayed application. Test importing the backup in a fresh profile. |

Record task success, interruptions, wrong turns and short verbatim observations with the participant's permission. Include a keyboard-only and screen-reader session in the Arabic interface. Check that mixed-direction questions, corrections, form controls and dialogue labels are read in a meaningful order.

## Delayed grammar practice

Pilot the sequence with *nachdem*, *bevor/bis*, and *seit/während*. Compare equally timed sessions using the same grammar and parallel prompts. Give one group the explanation and ordinary practice. Give the other the short attempt, targeted retry, and review sequence. Keep the time and amount of exposure comparable and record whether learners return.

Give both groups a short pretest and a parallel test seven days later. Hold back the test sentences from both groups' practice. The app's delayed items are learning activities for the second group. Use separate items to measure the outcome.

1. First Dalia printed an application. Then she signed it. Ask for one German sentence using *nachdem*.
2. The speaker will stay in the office until the technician arrives. Ask for a German message expressing the endpoint. Then ask for a sentence about closing the window before leaving.
3. Since Leon started a new job, he has taken the tram each day. Today he reads while he waits. Ask for two German sentences distinguishing the starting point and simultaneous actions.

Score the intended time relation, the connector, and the placement and tense of the verb independently. Allow grammatically valid alternatives, and anonymize submissions before a second rater checks a sample. Record hinted, self-rated and automatically checked responses separately. Compare the delayed results with the pretest and report the number who returned for the delayed test.

The audio exercise in the first exam model uses a locally synthesized German recording of an original announcement. Its three answer keys can be checked against the transcript included in the app.
